# Visual direction — specificity instead of generic AI-looking UI

**Read when:** a design is too templated, a redesign requires a differentiated direction, or a new product page lacks a coherent visual voice.

## Read the context

Identify audience, product job, brand tone, existing identity, actual content type, devices, competition and accessibility. Decide what should feel distinctive, trustworthy, quiet or expressive. Distinctive design is not synonymous with novelty: a utility interface may need deliberately unremarkable predictability.

## Diagnose symptoms, not authorship

A repetitive grid of three rounded feature cards, violet gradients, glass layers and oversized headlines can look generic *in combination with an unrelated brand*. These elements are not inherently wrong. Diagnose why each element exists and whether information architecture calls for a different composition. The goal is to eliminate undifferentiated choices, not to guess whether AI authored a page.

### Diagnostic questions

- Does hero copy state what the product actually does and for whom, without fabricated guarantees?
- Does the layout follow the real information hierarchy, or repeat a template regardless of content?
- Are illustrations, photos and icons meaningful and appropriately sourced/licensed?
- Is typographic scale derived from content and available viewport, not pasted from a showcase?
- Does the visual language reinforce actual brand assets, geographic setting and business trust?
- Are sections distinguishable by information role rather than random alternating backgrounds?
- Do states include mobile, overflow, empty, disabled, error and high-contrast/reduced-motion?

## Decision example

A maritime supply service should likely lead with real delivery conditions, clear order steps and trustworthy location/team photography, not a fictitious "AI automation platform" dashboard. A financial admin tool may appropriately choose simple tables rather than large editorial art direction.

## Creative freedom with checks

Select one coherent concept and at most one alternative when tradeoffs matter. Document typography hierarchy, content density, color usage, photo style, shape language and interaction philosophy. Deliberately constrain effects: two intentional visual accents are better than applying blur, shadow, bento and motion to every section.

## Review rubric

Evaluate specificity (product-specific facts), utility (content tasks are easy), craft (alignment/type/crop/spacing), trust (authentic claims/graphics), accessibility and coherence at multiple breakpoints. Give line/section-specific fixes rather than "make it premium."
