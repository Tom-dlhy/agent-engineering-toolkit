# Landing-page narrative, claims and proof

Read when: designing the content flow, hero message, product proof, objection-handling or audience-specific CTA of a public landing page.

## Evidence-led content brief
For each segment, write: audience and situation; pain/job; documented product capability; evidence source; obstacle; primary conversion action; unavailable facts. A strong hero says **what changes for the visitor**, for whom and how, with clear honest scope. Don't borrow a generic "revolutionize your business" heading because it looks premium.

```text
Audience: boat owner buying provisions for a scheduled port stop
Verified service: published ordering procedure and real contact channel
Hero question: Can I order in time and know where delivery happens?
Primary CTA: View ordering steps
Supporting proof: verified team/location/contact, no fabricated customer quotes
Known unknowns: exact delivery times, pricing conditions not supplied
```
This is an illustrative brief, not a factual assertion about a live merchant.

## Narrative design choices
Choose from hero, concrete demo, operational steps, compatible use cases, verifiable proof, objection handling, FAQ and CTA. Do not require every section for a simple local business; don't fabricate a review wall. Complex B2B buyers need workflow detail and comparison criteria; familiar local services may need price/process/contact assurance first.

Check hero clarity without animations and above the fold on narrow viewports. Test headings as real language, not lorem ipsum. Contrast against actual photography, not only a solid screenshot. Place legal constraints, privacy disclosure and honest availability near relevant commitments.

## Hand-off
`frontend-design` decides typography and composition; `content-strategy` handles multi-page editorial plans. This leaf owns one-page messaging integrity and source-to-claim traceability.

Relevant standards: https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/page-structure/ and https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum
