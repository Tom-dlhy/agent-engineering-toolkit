# Content strategy — answer real questions, own the maintenance

## Operational method
1. Identify real reader/customer tasks, positioning, product truth, local/regulatory context, approved examples, expertise sources and editorial ownership. Use permissioned first-party support/search signals and public research where relevant; never claim fictional volumes or personas.
2. Audit **current content** before creating another URL: route, topic/intent, audience, owner, freshness, performance evidence, conflicts, links and actual maintenance burden. Decide `KEEP / UPDATE / CONSOLIDATE / RETIRE / CREATE` at page level.
3. Map intent to outcome (understand/compare/decide/implement/troubleshoot); consolidate overlapping topics and specify truly differentiated proof (primary source, original example, verified process, dataset with permission). Do not produce hundreds of thin keyword variants.
4. Prioritize under explicit budget/subject-matter-review capacity. A small team with credible experts may benefit more from maintaining ten valuable pages than publishing a weekly filler calendar.
5. Specify content brief: reader question, claims/sources, outline/format, author/reviewer, factual/brand/legal approvals, primary next action, existing internal links, publication channel and refresh trigger.
6. Define a lifecycle from research to review, publish, monitor, refresh or withdraw. Editorial publishing, customer communications and legal claims require user authorization and, where necessary, human review.
7. Measure audience benefit and qualified outcomes with declared denominator/window/consent limits; do not equate pageviews with success, social likes with conversions, or correlation with causation.

## Explicit non-ownership
`technical-seo` handles HTTP crawl/index and structured data; `design/landing-page-design` handles landing conversion composition; `design/content-design` owns product microcopy; `lifecycle-retention` owns permissioned CRM/email/push journeys; `growth-experimentation` owns randomization, tests and decision gates. You may collaborate but avoid a second source of truth. Exit with a source-backed inventory, publication/maintenance decisions, owners, cost and testable outcomes, not an uncontrolled content factory.
