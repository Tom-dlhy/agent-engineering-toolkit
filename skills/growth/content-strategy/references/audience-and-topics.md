# Audience and topic architecture

**Read when:** researching audience needs and selecting which subjects merit authoritative content. Skip for routine copy polishing or crawler troubleshooting.

## Begin with reality

Prefer permissioned first-party evidence: de-identified support tickets, user interviews, search-in-site terms, actual objections and product docs. Secondary sources: public competitor material and search demand tools, clearly marked as approximate. Do not fabricate search volumes or customer interviews.

## Audience / intent mapping

| Audience | Situation | Real question | Value to deliver | Format |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| First-time customer | Understanding a service | What does this deliver and for whom? | Specific limits and examples | FAQ or explainer |
| Returning customer | Completing a job | Which steps, data and timing are needed? | Correct workflow and checklist | Guide |
| Technical evaluator | Assessing risks | How is identity / data protected? | Evidence, architecture and limitations | Technical article |

Adapt the rows to actual users and available evidence. Group search intent into learn, compare, evaluate, purchase, implement, and troubleshoot. A topic cluster should answer genuinely adjacent questions, not merely permute keywords.

## Method: create a content map

1. Collect verbatim customer questions and remove identifiers.
2. Tag audience, stage, importance, friction, existing answer and source confidence.
3. Consolidate duplicate intents; split materially different intent even if vocabulary overlaps.
4. Choose a pillar with a clear core promise, supported by narrowly scoped explanatory pages; map parent-child internal links where useful.
5. Estimate value as audience frequency × strategic consequence × evidence quality relative to writing and maintenance cost. This is qualitative until real measurements exist.
6. Prioritize refreshing an existing strong page ahead of creating a competing near-duplicate.
7. Create a brief listing approved sources and the unique demonstration or dataset that makes this page worth reading.

## What differentiates content

An observed failure mode, practical walkthrough, realistic constraints, executable example, original measurement or verified policy adds value. A generic rewrite of public documentation without analysis often does not. Choose format appropriate to the job: comparison table for decisions; instructions for workflows; problem/solution narrative for case studies.

## Pitfalls and tests

Test the topic against a real customer question: can the proposed outline answer it correctly, including exceptions and next steps? Does it cannibalize an existing URL? Would publication imply unsupported superiority, outcomes or testimonials? Do sources still match the current product?
